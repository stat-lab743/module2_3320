A score needs a reference point.

Dashed line: mean μSolid line + dot: raw score X

Horizontal axis: raw scores. Tick labels show μ and steps of 1 SD. The view expands when needed; curve height is rescaled for readability. This bell curve is illustrative—z scores do not require normally distributed data.

Standardized score
z = (X − μ) / σ

Use the sliders, arrow keys, or typed numbers. σ must be greater than 0. For a participant’s word count, choose a whole number. All measures and reference groups are fictional teaching examples.

Change one thing. Watch what happens. First predict, then change X, μ, or σ. Compare the sign, distance from the mean, and size of z. Keep the other two values fixed to isolate an effect.

03 / Your Canvas paper

Keep the evidence.
Explain the meaning.

Use these seven headings in your paper. Record the measure, X, μ, σ, and z for each trial you discuss. Report z to two decimal places.

  1. Part 1 · Explore a z Score

    Start with memory: X = 35, μ = 30, σ = 5. Interpret the sign and number of SDs away. Choose X below μ, record it, and explain what changed.

  2. Part 2 · Change the Distribution

    Start with X = 38, μ = 30, σ = 5. Keep X and μ fixed; choose a different σ. Record both z scores. Why did z change when X did not?

  3. Part 3 · Same Score, Different Meaning

    Memory X = 60 in both studies. A: μ = 50, σ = 10. B: μ = 58, σ = 4. Calculate both z scores. Does the same raw score mean the same relative standing?

  4. Part 4 · Compare Psychological Measures

    Maya’s memory: 38, 30, 5. Attention: 88, 70, 10 (X, μ, σ). Which is farther above its mean? Why can’t 38 and 88 alone answer this?

  5. Part 5 · Does Negative Mean Bad?

    Reaction time: 325, 400, 50. Stress: 30, 20, 5 (X, μ, σ). Interpret each z. Evaluate “negative means poor performance” and “positive means desirable.”

  6. Part 6 · Experiment

    Create +1.25 ≤ z ≤ +2.25 and record all values. Change only one value to reach −0.50 ≤ z ≤ +0.50. Record your new values and explain why z moved toward zero.

  7. Part 7 · Data-to-Decision

    A’s memory: 42, 30, 6. B’s attention: 94, 70, 10 (X, μ, σ). Evaluate: “B is more unusual because 94 is much larger than 42.” Assess the conclusion and reasoning separately; use z scores as evidence.

Before submitting: Include your own chosen values and observations, explain changes in context, and follow your instructor’s paper requirements. Submit the separate paper in Canvas.
